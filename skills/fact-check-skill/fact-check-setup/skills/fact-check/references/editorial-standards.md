# BlueFox editorial standards

Reference for why `scripts/style_check.py` flags what it flags. Read this if a flag seems wrong or you need to explain one of the fixes.

## No em-dashes
BlueFox copy uses commas, periods, or parentheses instead of em-dashes (—). It's a deliberate voice choice, not a typo fix.

## Sentence case headers
Headers should read "Set up your DMARC record", not "Set Up Your DMARC Record". Only proper nouns and acronyms (DMARC, SES, SDK) keep their capitals.

## "Small team," not "solo founder"
Gyula runs BlueFox with a small team, not alone. Content should never frame the company as a solo operation, even if that was once informally true — it undersells the current team and reads as less credible to prospective customers evaluating reliability.

## Avoid defensive or overreaching language
Claims like "we guarantee," "no other provider," "impossible to beat," or "the only tool that" invite easy rebuttal and read as insecure rather than confident. Prefer specific, checkable claims ("supports X, which Y and Z don't as of [date]") over absolute ones. This ties directly into the fact-check itself: an absolute claim is also the hardest kind to keep true over time.

## Verify against primary sources
This is the standard the whole skill exists to enforce. A comparison article is only as good as its weakest verified claim — one wrong number in a pricing table undermines the credibility of the whole piece, even if everything else is airtight.
